Vivid · Brand Architecture · Logo System Proposals · v0.2

Four Marks. Four Directions.

Each candidate is a designed mark — geometric shapes, intentional construction — not a typed word in a font. The mark carries the brand. The wordmark is treated quietly so it can flex across the family. These are directional concepts; once a direction is picked, custom letterform execution should be done by a designer in vector tooling.

What changed from v0.1

v0.1 was Montserrat and Oswald typed as text — typography, not logo design. v0.2 builds each candidate around an actual SVG mark with constructed geometry. The mark is the work. The wordmark is intentionally restrained so the mark can carry the brand recognition.

01 Apex
Motorsport geometry · Asymmetric V

A V drawn as a racing apex. The two legs are intentionally different weights — the left thicker, the right tapering — suggesting entry, apex, exit. Pure geometry; reads as motorsport without any literal racing iconography. Negative space inside the V is a clean triangle that recurs in the wordmark.

What it gets right

The asymmetric V is a real piece of design work — readable as a brand mark even at favicon size. The triangle motif from the V's negative space recurs in the wordmark, tying mark and word together. Heritage Red carries through. Reads as motorsport without being literal.

Trade-off

Asymmetric geometry won't survive a bad redraw. Requires a designer to refine the curve where the legs meet at the apex and tune the taper proportions. Don't ship until the mark is in vector and grid-checked.

02 Stripe
Racing-stripe minimal · Cut-through V

A solid V with a horizontal racing-stripe cut through the middle. The stripe is the signature — appears in the mark, recurs as a separator in the wordmark lockup, can become a UI element across product pages and creative. Maximum restraint, minimum decoration.

What it gets right

The stripe is a signature device that extends past the logo into the rest of the brand — dividers, callouts, page rules, section separators. The mark and the playbook design system reinforce each other. Reads as restrained and modern.

Trade-off

Generic risk: many performance brands use a stripe motif. The execution has to be confident — exact stripe weight, exact V proportions, exact placement — to feel like Vivid and not a tier-2 catalog house. Less distinctive at favicon size.

03 Crest
Marque heritage · Shield + V

A geometric shield housing a sharp V. Reads as motorsport heritage in the Porsche / AMG / Akrapovič register — pedigree as design. The shield outline carries across sub-brands; the inner glyph swaps. Strongest "this is a serious marque" signal of the four.

What it gets right

The shield + inner-glyph system gives every sub-brand its own crest while preserving family identity. Strongest signal of pedigree. Works embossed on apparel, foil-stamped on print, embroidered on jackets. The system scales beyond logos into product detailing.

Trade-off

Heaviest of the four. Crests can read as fussy or pretentious if the execution is even slightly off. Doesn't work as well in tight digital spaces (favicon, social avatar) where the shield outline gets lost. Needs an alternate compact lockup.

04 Hex
Modular system · Hex container + family glyph

A hexagon — the shape of a fastener, a billet machined face — houses a swappable family glyph. The hex is the constant: it identifies "Vivid" anywhere it appears. The glyph inside changes by vertical. Most modular system of the four — and the only one designed from day one to scale to a fifth, sixth, seventh sub-brand without retooling.

What it gets right

Most operationally flexible system. Each sub-brand has its own glyph in the hex, but the hex shape is the family signature. Adding a fifth vertical later (Vivid Marine, Vivid Track, whatever) takes one new glyph, not a new logo system. The hex also reads as "mechanical / engineered" — a callback to fasteners and billet aluminum that fits performance parts naturally.

Trade-off

Hexagon is a common shape in tech / SaaS — risk that the brand reads as software rather than performance parts. Mitigated by the strong Heritage Red and the swappable glyph, but worth eye-testing against competitors. Inner glyph craft has to be tight — sloppy T or C glyphs will undermine the whole system.
